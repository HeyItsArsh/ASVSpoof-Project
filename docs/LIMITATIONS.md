# Known limitations

- ASVspoof 2019 LA covers particular TTS/VC methods and recording conditions. New neural generators, codecs, microphones, languages, accents and adversarial transformations can fail differently. It does not establish resistance to physical replay or all authentication attacks.
- A binary spoof score does not verify identity, liveness, consent, human speech or freshness. An attacker may replay bona fide audio. Authentication requires additional independent controls and security assessment.
- EER is an offline operating-point summary. Accuracy depends on the chosen dev threshold and prevalence. Deployment costs/priors differ from the dataset. Primary ASVspoof 2019 minimum t-DCF is not implemented.
- Legacy reference EER partially orders equal scores, so exact ties can give misleading values. We preserve reference behavior, count ties and also report a grouped/interpolated diagnostic EER. Operational dev thresholds are attainable grouped ROC thresholds.
- Fixed center crops can omit localized artifacts. Zero padding introduces duration cues. Augmentation is a bundled gain/noise/random-crop treatment, so its effects cannot be assigned to one transform without further ablations. Linear Mel may be harder to optimize under the same learning rate; report failed/unstable runs rather than hiding them.
- MobileNetV2's depthwise operations may be slower than expected on some CPUs. From-scratch training on all three seeds plus ablations can take days on a CPU laptop. Lower training budgets change the experiment and must be declared before eval.
- MFCC statistics discard temporal structure. RBF SVM training and prediction can be costly as the support-vector count grows. Deterministic baselines may show identical results across seeds.
- Three seeds provide a small estimate of training variability, not confidence about future speakers/generators. Speaker/attack-aware uncertainty requires additional resampling analysis that is not included here.
- Silence/short/corrupt audio is rejected. Noise produces finite model outputs plus a heuristic warning, but there is no validated speech/OOD detector. A model may confidently label arbitrary noise bona fide. No classifier output here grants authentication.
- Synthetic mechanics tests use generated tones and cannot validate generalisation. The actual tiny-set check is only a training-loop diagnostic, with explicit tiny-set BatchNorm calibration. All three systems have shuffled-label controls; passing them does not prove absence of every possible leakage mechanism.
- Windows SAPI is a local, reproducible convenience probe, not representative of recent neural TTS. Voice inventories differ across computers. A spoof-only probe cannot support EER or false-rejection claims; a matched consented bona fide collection is needed.
- Audit speaker IDs are those in the official protocol, not a new independent identity investigation. Decoded exact-content hashes detect copies across splits but not near duplicates, re-encoding with altered samples or all possible source-utterance relationships.
- Deterministic operations and seeds improve reproducibility in the pinned environment; floating-point kernels and results can still vary across devices and library versions. Cache identity uses source metadata; re-audit and clear caches after deliberate metadata-preserving file changes.
- Full training resume within an epoch is not implemented. Completed runs can be skipped with `train --resume`; incomplete runs preserve a diagnostic checkpoint and restart from their seed. Frozen manifests are an experiment audit aid, not protection against intentional tampering.
- Do not deploy this university prototype as a sole security control. Evaluate false accept/reject harms, privacy, consent, and demographic/domain performance with appropriate stakeholders before any real deployment.
